Alabama Construction Stormwater Permit Analysis

Seventeen years of NPDES Construction General Permit (ALR100000) coverage, inspection records, and enforcement actions from the Alabama Department of Environmental Management eFile system
Prepared for: Mobile Baykeeper
Coverage period: June 8, 2009 through April 27, 2026
Data source: ADEM eFile public portal (app.adem.alabama.gov/eFile) and Laserfiche WebLink document archive (lf.adem.alabama.gov/WebLink)
Records analyzed: 123,424 permit documents · 22,325 unique construction sites · 33,205 inspection reports
Report date: April 28, 2026
This analysis was made possible by automated public-records collection at scale. Mobile Baykeeper has long sought a longitudinal view of NPDES Construction General Permit coverage and enforcement across Alabama. The dataset behind this report — every electronically filed ALR10-prefix document since June 2009 — was assembled, parsed, and analyzed in approximately 24 hours of compute time using open-source tooling. The underlying data is public; the bottleneck has always been the scale of records to process. The full per-site, per-company, and per-inspection CSVs accompany this report.

Across the seventeen years of digital records, Alabama's Department of Environmental Management has issued roughly 1,200 to 1,750 new construction-stormwater permits per year. New-permit volume has held remarkably steady: there is no evidence of a building boom in the data, only modest year-over-year fluctuation. What has changed dramatically over the same period is the regulator's follow-through. Inspection rates have fallen from 70% in 2012 to 27% in 2025; formal enforcement actions have collapsed from 25% to 5% of new sites. Inspectors who do visit sites are finding more violations, not fewer — the in-writing problem-citation rate has climbed from 29% in 2011 to 65% in recent years. The conclusion is unavoidable: ADEM's regulatory intensity is being cut by attrition, not by improving site conditions.

The Regulatory Decline: Statewide Trend

The single chart below contains the structural finding behind every per-builder statistic in this report.

From 2012 to 2025, the inspection rate (green) and enforcement rate (orange) both fell by roughly two-thirds. Over the same window, the rate at which inspectors who do visit a site cite a BMP problem in writing (red) has climbed from under 30% to over 60%. ADEM's field staff are documenting more violations per inspection while the agency files dramatically fewer enforcement actions in response.

Mobile Bay Watershed: Mobile and Baldwin Counties

Mobile Bay-specific finding: 4,223 construction sites started in Mobile and Baldwin counties since 2009. 2,173 of them had inspector-cited BMP violations. Only 794 received formal enforcement. 1,594 sites in the Mobile Bay watershed have documented violations and no enforcement on record. That is the local equivalent of the statewide ADEM gap.

Mobile Bay's inspection rate has historically been higher than the state average — typically 75-90% through 2022, well above the statewide 50-70%. This may reflect the higher density of permitted sites in the coastal counties (easier to reach) or higher inspector staffing in the Mobile Field Operations Division. However, Mobile Bay has tracked the same downward trend in recent years, falling from 88% inspection coverage in 2014 to 49% in 2025. The enforcement rate has dropped from a peak of 30% in 2012-2013 to 7% in 2025.

Methodology

Data sources. Two ADEM public web systems were queried: the eFile search portal at app.adem.alabama.gov/eFile and the Laserfiche WebLink document archive at lf.adem.alabama.gov/WebLink. The eFile portal was queried year-by-year from June 8, 2009 (system inception) through April 27, 2026, filtering on Water media area and the ALR10 permit prefix that identifies NPDES Construction General Permit coverages. All 123,424 returned document records were captured.

Site count. Each unique permit number with at least one Notice of Intent (NOI) document is counted as one construction site. The "start year" is the year of the earliest NOI filing on that permit, accounting for amendments and re-filings that would otherwise inflate counts.

Inspection extraction. All 33,326 INSPR-type documents on permits with NOIs were downloaded individually from Laserfiche, parsed in memory, and discarded. Three parser paths were used depending on document format: PDF documents (the post-2018 AEPACS standard) via LiteParse OCR; Microsoft PowerPoint and Word Open XML documents (common pre-2018) via direct Python parsing; and legacy Compound Document format via LibreOffice round-trip conversion. 33,205 inspections (99.6%) parsed successfully. The remaining 121 documents were unreachable on the Laserfiche server.

BMP categorization. Inspector narrative text and inspection-form photo captions were scanned for keyword phrases identifying the five BMP categories listed above, with a context window check for negative-finding language ("not maintained", "inadequate", "absent", "failing", etc.) to distinguish a cited problem from a routine mention. This is a high-precision, conservative categorization — it counts only inspector-written findings, not inferred violations.

Enforcement. A site is counted as "enforced" if any of three formal enforcement document types appear on the permit: Notice of Violation (ENOV), Enforcement Warning Letter (EWL), or Enforcement Action Order (EAO). Routine correspondence and engineering reports are not counted as enforcement.

What this analysis does not capture. Self-reported inspections that did not result in formal ADEM filings; informal inspector-permittee communications outside the document record; verbal warnings; settlements that did not generate a public document; pre-2009 paper-era inspections. The analysis also does not independently verify the inspector's findings — a future "vision verification" pass could compare inspection photos against the inspector's written conclusions.

Reproducibility. All raw scraping output (rows_YYYY.jsonl), per-site CSVs, per-company CSVs, per-year trend CSVs, and the inspection text-extraction CSVs are preserved alongside this report. Re-running the analysis from scratch on the same window of data should produce identical numbers.
This report was generated as part of an exploratory data analysis project. It is not a formal regulatory or legal document. All conclusions about ADEM staffing, enforcement intent, or builder behavior are based on patterns in the public document record alone.
